# Benefit Atlas pilot: moderator-only answer key

Use this to score the [three fictional tasks](moderator-guide.md), not to advise a member about their own coverage. The [NJC PSHCP Directive](https://www.njc-cnm.gc.ca/directive/d9/en) controls. The scenarios were spot-checked against the live Directive and the [Canada Life Member Booklet (November 2026)](https://www.welcome.canadalife.com/content/dam/rfp/welcome-sites/pshcp/PSHCP-member-booklet.pdf) on November 23, 2026. Recheck the sources if sessions are delayed or the documents change.

| Task | Benefit | Key condition | Official next step |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S — Sore feet** | Names at least one relevant category to explore: podiatry/chiropody, orthotics, or orthopaedic shoes. Does not diagnose or present a service as automatically covered. | Gives one accurate condition for the category named. Podiatry, chiropody and eligible foot care by a nurse at a community nursing station share a **$500 eligible-expense limit per calendar year**. Orthotics: **one eligible pair per calendar year**, including repairs, with a written prescription and an eligible dispenser. Orthopaedic shoes: **$250 in eligible expenses per calendar year**; qualifying shoes are integral to a brace or specially constructed for the patient (including modifications to those shoes), and require a written prescription. | Check the plan rules and confirm the intended service or item and provider or supplier with [Canada Life](https://www.welcome.canadalife.com/pshcp) before spending. |
| **M — Massage and physiotherapy** | Identifies both benefits and says **the limits are separate**. | Per person, per calendar year: **$500 eligible expenses for massage** and **$1,500 eligible expenses for physiotherapy**. These are not payouts; at 80% the respective reimbursements can be up to $400 and $1,200, subject to eligibility and reasonable and customary charges. Exact payout examples are optional if the expense-versus-payment distinction is clear. | Check the remaining balances and provider eligibility with [Canada Life](https://www.welcome.canadalife.com/pshcp) before booking. |
| **C — CPAP replacement** | Identifies the CPAP/BiPAP **device** benefit, not the separate parts and servicing benefit. | Identifies the **60-month** rule (prior eligible purchases or rentals of CPAP/BiPAP or qualifying dental appliances can reduce what’s eligible) and says to check the prescription and other required medical documentation. Don’t require a new sleep study as a universal answer. Parts and servicing have a separate $500 eligible-expense cap per calendar year; this is optional context, not a machine replacement allowance. | Contact [Canada Life](https://www.welcome.canadalife.com/pshcp) before ordering to confirm the next eligible date, exact device and supplier, and documents needed. An itemized quote and predetermination are useful next steps, not a guarantee of payment. |

**Source references:** [Directive §6.4, Medical Practitioners Benefit, and §6.5, Miscellaneous Expense Benefit](https://www.njc-cnm.gc.ca/directive/d9/v283/s832/en) and [§10, Summary of Maximum Eligible Expenses](https://www.njc-cnm.gc.ca/directive/d9/v283/s836/en). Booklet printed pages: **S** 41, 43, 81; **M** 40–41, 81; **C** 27–28, 49, 53–54, 85.

## Known differences to watch during the pilot

- Atlas’s guided finder may return no match for “massage and physio,” even though both benefits are in the directory. Observe whether members recover without a hint.
- Atlas labels the orthotics card “Custom orthotics,” while the official heading is “Orthotics.” Don’t infer that all off-the-shelf inserts are eligible or ineligible from either heading.
- Atlas’s CPAP card says “1 purchase”; the details and official sources also cover qualifying rentals. Score against the official rule. Record confusion and clarify it in the debrief, not mid-task.
