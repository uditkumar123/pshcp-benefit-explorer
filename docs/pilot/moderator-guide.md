# Benefit Atlas pilot: moderator guide

**Goal:** Can members who don’t know what to look up find the right benefit, understand a key condition, and identify an official next step? Compare Benefit Atlas with the [PSHCP Administration Authority coverage guide](https://pshcp.ca/coverage/). Correctness matters more than speed.

**Setup:** Five sessions, about 25 minutes each. Use the [recruitment invitation](recruitment.md) and review the [moderator-only answer key](answer-key.md) before the first session. Open each task at the assigned site’s home or coverage page in a fresh tab. Atlas now splits its content across separate pages (benefits, practitioners, finder, usage, claims); start at its home page and allow normal navigation. Allow normal navigation, site search, and public source links; no outside search, account sign-in, or claim submission. Use the same device and browser for both sites in each session.

## Read aloud

“We’re testing the websites, not you. These situations are fictional. Please don’t share personal, medical, plan, or claim details. You can skip a task or stop at any time. Is it okay if I take brief notes? As you go, please tell me what you’re looking for and what you think the page means.”

## Tasks and order

- **S — Sore feet:** “Jordan’s feet are sore after long shifts and they don’t know what benefit to look up. What might they check under the plan? Find one important condition and say where they should verify coverage before spending.”
- **M — Massage and physiotherapy:** “Sam is considering both massage therapy and physiotherapy this year. Do they use the same limit? Find the two limits, explain what those amounts mean, and say where Sam should check before booking.”
- **C — CPAP replacement:** “Alex is considering replacing a CPAP machine, not just its supplies. Find what affects the replacement, what documents to check, and what Alex should verify before ordering.”

Each member attempts each scenario once, so prior answers don’t carry over to the same task on the other site. Don’t give answers between tasks.

## Observe and decide

Start timing when navigation starts. Use neutral prompts: “What are you looking for now?” Stop when the member says they’re done or after four minutes. Ask: “What did you find? What could change the answer? What would you check next, and where? What did you see that supports that?”

With permission, record one row per attempt in private notes:

`Member ID | Device | Site/order | Task | Benefit | Key condition | Official next step | Time | Help given | Friction or misunderstanding`

Score each of the three answer components **correct / partial / incorrect / not found**. Track assistance separately. A fully successful attempt has all three correct without help. Compare speed only across fully successful attempts on the **same scenario**, and treat it as descriptive. At the end, ask: “Where were you least sure, and what would you do in real life?”

After five sessions, look for repeated, unassisted success on Atlas, especially on the unnamed-benefit task. Report observations by scenario and site, not a statistical winner. If members repeatedly get stuck, fix that path and retest. A confidently wrong answer that could lead to spending money, or an accessibility barrier that blocks completion, is a fix-before-wider-sharing finding. If Atlas shows no clear advantage, pause expansion.
